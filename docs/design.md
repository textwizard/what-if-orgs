## Design record (final setup)

This section records the design as it stands, with the reason for each choice, as the basis for the technical appendix. The chronological log below keeps the history.

### Framing
The platform builds one reference organization per real firm, a language-model agent that combines the anchors of the firm (its context), a mix of behavioral theories (its reasoning), and the memory of its earlier decisions and their results. The mix is calibrated on the firm's own decisions and validated on later decisions. What-if organizations are differently endowed versions of a reference organization, with other anchors or another mix. The hot stove effect of Denrell and March (2001) is one example application. The unit of analysis is the single firm, which distinguishes the design from the aggregate validation of the working paper.

### Periods
Calibration uses new products launched in 2018 and 2019, with the firm described by its website of 2017. Validation uses new products launched in 2023 and 2024, with the firm described by its website of 2022, and decisions observed until 2026, so that a product of 2024 can be seen to disappear in 2025 and stay absent in 2026. The pandemic years 2020 and 2021 lie between the two periods, so that neither description of a firm falls into them and the validation tests whether calibrated mixes carry over a structural break. Common Crawl holds few pages of these firms before 2017, which rules out earlier context years. Each description precedes the products it concerns, so that the agent knows only what was knowable before the decision.

### Sample
Candidates come from the sample frame of the working paper (data/closed/sample.csv), without wholesale, retail, publishing, media, advertising, staffing, and associations, and with at least eight years covered by the archive. Firms are eligible with at least 15 archived pages in 2017, 2022, and each year that carries the trajectories of products, at least three product pages in each window, and at least three news pages in each window, since firms report the results of their products there (code/48_select_150.py). Well-known brands, domains of a different entity, and associations are excluded, since the model would recognize them or they are not firms. The archive screen proceeds as a funnel. A wide screen of the Common Crawl index (three screens, the third drawn from the upper tertile of the web-based innovation probability of the panel, since the design needs firms that launch products), a single cheap coding of products with gpt-5.4-mini, and a final selection of the firms with at least three new products in each window and statements of results or problems about any of their products in each window, stratified by technological regime (code/51_select_final.py). Results stated for the new products themselves are too rare for a selection rule (3 to 4% of new products). The selection uses no information on later drops, so that it does not select on the outcome.

### Anchors
Pages are selected by role with fixed quotas per firm and year, including career and research pages, which carry signals of the firm (whatif/web.py, config.yaml). The situation of the firm is coded from up to 30,000 characters per year, with imprint, home, about, research, career, and news pages before the offer. Each signal requires concrete, current, and firm-specific evidence, with a strength of 2 or 3 and a quote that appears verbatim in the pages (protocols/context.md). History, advertising language, menu labels, and the products the firm sells are excluded, since a first coding showed that about half of the signals rested on such text. Signals the website does not mention are left out of the description, since silence on a website is no evidence that a firm lacks something. The industry is described by Eurostat short-term statistics and the Community Innovation Survey, and participation in EU research projects by CORDIS. Anchors are derived from the constructs of the chosen theories, so that every theory finds evidence in the description that its reasoning can combine with the theory text. The theory of value of the firm is coded in 2017 and 2022.

### Coding
Each firm-year is coded from up to 45,000 characters, which reach most news and product pages of a year (14,000 reached 13% of the news pages). All anchors, products, and result statements are coded by two models, Claude Opus 5.5 and gpt-5.4, and the agreement between them is reported. A signal enters the description where both coders find it or where the evidence is specific and verbatim.

### Products, results, and decisions
New products and their prominence on the website are coded per year. A product is dropped when it disappears from the website for good, which reveals a decision of the firm. Results are what the firm states about a product, such as sales figures, awards, adoption by customers, or new versions. A launch is not a result. Firms rarely report failures. Silence about a product was tested as evidence of failure and is not: a stated result does not lower the chance that a product is dropped, whereas a stated problem raises it strongly and the prominence of a product on the website has a clear gradient (dropped 15% when only named, 8% with a page of its own, 5% on the home page). The early signals the agent sees are therefore the stated results and problems and the lagged prominence of each product, and silence is left as silence.

### What the agent sees
In calibration and validation, the agent decides about the real products of its firm. Each year it sees the results stated on the website and the prominence of each product in the years before the decision, but never its presence in the year being predicted, since that is the outcome. Assigned results, with a known potential and a first result pushed toward failure or success, are used only for the instrument checks and for what-if experiments, since they show how theories respond to results we control but do not correspond to the results the real firm saw.

### Theories
The reference organizations mix the behavioral theory of the firm, absorptive capacity, escalation of commitment, real options reasoning, and no theory. These theories address whether a firm keeps or abandons a new product after early results and have an established discourse in organization science and strategy. The behavioral theory of the firm and absorptive capacity alone both drop more products than the real firms do, so no mixture of them could match the observed persistence, whereas escalation of commitment and real options reasoning predict more persistence. Experiential learning is represented by the rule-based twin of Denrell and March. Theory texts use the version of considerations, which states what management weighs without stating the decision. In the instrument checks across six models, the version that states the decision separated the theories in every model but scripts the answer, whereas naming the concepts or describing all mechanisms did not separate them reliably.

### Calibration and validation
Weights are estimated at three levels, common to all firms, per technological regime or size class, and per firm, shrunk toward the common weights, since two to six products per firm would otherwise fit noise. The aggregate prediction is the mean of the firm predictions. Validation compares the predictions after the pandemic with the recorded keep and drop decisions of each firm and with the abandonment rates of the Community Innovation Survey by industry and size class, against the agent without theory and the common weights. Coherence between the weights and the theory of value stated on the website is examined in both periods, and changes of the theory of value between 2017 and 2022 are related to changes of the weights.

### Language models
All calls go through the batch interfaces of the providers, at half the price, and are stored in a cache under the hash of model, settings, and prompt, so that every result can be traced and repeated at no cost (whatif/llm.py). DeepSeek has no batch interface and is called in its hours of reduced prices. Responses are not stored by OpenAI. The models of the final simulation are chosen once the sample is fixed. The models were trained after both periods, so their knowledge of the firms is checked with a re-identification test and a recall test of the fate of products, instead of relying on a knowledge cutoff before the validation period.

## Silence as evidence and figure v3 (2026-10-14)
- `code/23_silence.py` -> `analysis/signals/product_year.csv`, `silence_firm_year.csv`. ## 2026-10-15: design change, dogmatic vs revisable dropped

The factor "how the theory is held" (dogmatic or revisable with a stated refutation criterion) was removed from the design at the user's request. It had been introduced by us, not in the original brief, and in the toy a pre-stated test made all theories of innovation end at the same values, which works against the claim that theory content directs reasoning. The application is now: how do what-if organizations respond to early failure and early success of an innovation, and how does this depend on the theory of innovation and its fit with the firm's theory of value. Hypothesis: knowledge-based theories (KPF, AC) reduce abandonment of promising innovations after early failure but increase retention of poor ones after early success, BTF the opposite, stronger with fit. Design: 26 distinct conditions per firm (theory 7 x information 2 x reasoning 2, collapsed for no theory), first result (failure or success) assigned within runs, about 170,000 calls.
