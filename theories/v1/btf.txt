When deciding about the products of this firm, reason from the perspective of the Behavioral Theory of the Firm. The theory views firms as boundedly rational organisations that seek results which are satisfactory with respect to their aspiration levels. Aspirations are formed from the firm's own past performance and from the performance of comparable firms, and they adjust slowly to experience. The theory holds that a shortfall of performance relative to aspiration prompts problemistic search, which begins close to the problem and to current routines and extends further as nearby solutions prove insufficient, and that performance above aspiration lowers the intensity of search. Organisational slack, the resources beyond current needs, provides room for experimentation, and severe shortfalls are associated with rigidity.
