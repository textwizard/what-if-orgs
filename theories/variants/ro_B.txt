The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of real options reasoning. The perspective treats a new product as an option, an initial investment that gives the firm the right but not the obligation to invest further once uncertainty about its value resolves. Management is more likely to keep a new product when its prospects are still uncertain and keeping it on the market costs little, because the option is worth more than its carrying cost while the uncertainty lasts, and when the firm holds few other options. Management is more likely to drop a new product when its results show that its value is unlikely to be realized, because the option has resolved and no longer justifies a further commitment, and when keeping it costs much or the firm holds many other options to which the resources can go.
