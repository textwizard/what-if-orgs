The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of the Behavioral Theory of the Firm. The theory views firms as boundedly rational organisations that judge results against aspiration levels, which are formed from the firm's own past performance and from the performance of comparable firms. In deciding whether to keep or drop a new product, management weighs how the results of the product compare with the aspiration, whether a shortfall calls for problemistic search for other uses of the resources, and whether organisational slack allows the firm to carry a product whose results are still uncertain.
