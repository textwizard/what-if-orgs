The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of escalation of commitment. The perspective holds that decision makers tend to persist with a course of action into which they have invested resources and for which they are responsible, even when its results disappoint, because costs already incurred weigh on the decision, because earlier decisions call for justification, and because the firm has committed itself in public. In deciding whether to keep or drop a new product, management weighs how much the firm has invested in the product, how visibly it has committed to the product, how central the product is to the direction the firm has announced, and whether disappointing results can still be attributed to temporary causes.
