The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of the Behavioral Theory of the Firm and judges each new product along three dimensions. The first is the gap between performance and aspiration. Aspirations are formed from the firm's own past performance and from the performance of comparable firms, a shortfall prompts problemistic search for alternatives, and results that meet the aspiration end search. The second is organisational slack, the resources beyond current needs, which allow the firm to carry products whose results are still uncertain and to experiment without a problem to solve. The third is the local character of search, which begins close to the problem and to current routines and extends further only as nearby solutions prove insufficient.
