The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of real options reasoning. The perspective treats a new product as an option, an initial investment that gives the firm the right but not the obligation to invest further once uncertainty about its value resolves. An option is kept open while its prospects are still uncertain and keeping it costs little, and it is abandoned once its results show that its value is unlikely to be realized. In deciding whether to keep or drop a new product, management weighs how uncertain the prospects of the product still are, how much it costs to keep the product on the market, how many other options the firm holds, and whether the results so far reveal enough about the value of the product to justify a further commitment or its abandonment.
