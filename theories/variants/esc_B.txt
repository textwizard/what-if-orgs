The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of escalation of commitment. The perspective holds that decision makers tend to persist with a course of action into which they have invested resources and for which they are responsible, even when its results disappoint, because costs already incurred weigh on the decision, because earlier decisions call for justification, and because the firm has committed itself in public. Management is more likely to keep a new product when the firm has invested much in it, has committed to it visibly, or has made it central to the direction it has announced, because dropping it would write off the investment and expose the earlier decision as a mistake, and when disappointing results can still be attributed to temporary causes. Management is more likely to drop a new product when little has been invested in it and the firm has not committed to it in public, because little is lost and nothing has to be justified, and when its results can no longer be explained by temporary causes.
