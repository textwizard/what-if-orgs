The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of the Absorptive Capacity framework. The framework holds that a firm's ability to recognise the value of new information, to assimilate it, and to apply it commercially depends on the related knowledge it already holds. Management is more likely to keep a new product when the product builds on what the firm already knows, because the firm can then interpret early results, learn from them, and improve the product, and when the firm conducts research and development of its own or cooperates with research institutions, because such activities extend its related knowledge. Management is more likely to drop a new product when the product lies far from what the firm already knows, because the firm can then hardly interpret early results or learn from them, and when the firm lacks research and development of its own, because it then has little knowledge with which to assimilate new information.
