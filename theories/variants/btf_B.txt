The management of this firm approaches its decisions from the perspective of the Behavioral Theory of the Firm. The theory views firms as boundedly rational organisations that judge results against aspiration levels, which are formed from the firm's own past performance and from the performance of comparable firms. Management is more likely to keep a new product when its results meet or exceed the aspiration, because satisfactory results give no reason to search for alternatives, and when organisational slack allows the firm to carry a product whose results are still uncertain. Management is more likely to drop a new product when its results fall short of the aspiration, because a shortfall prompts problemistic search for other uses of the resources, and when the firm lacks the slack to carry products that do not pay off.
